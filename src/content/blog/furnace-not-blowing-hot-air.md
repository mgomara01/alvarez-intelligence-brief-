It's the first genuinely cold night of the year, you've cranked the thermostat, and your furnace is enthusiastically blowing… room-temperature disappointment. A **furnace not blowing hot air** is the HVAC equivalent of a kettle that boils everything except your tea — and the fix is often a two-minute, zero-dollar checklist before you ever pick up the phone.

Grab a flashlight. Let's walk it.

## Check 1: the thermostat (yes, really)

Before anything dramatic, check the obvious:

- Is it set to **HEAT**, and is the target temp above the current room temp?
- Is the fan on **AUTO**, not **ON**? Set to ON, the blower runs *constantly* — including between heat cycles — so you feel cool air and assume the worst. Switch it to AUTO.
- Fresh **batteries**? A dying thermostat throws weird, intermittent gremlins.

This single check resolves a shocking number of "my furnace is broken" calls. No shame — we've all done it.

## Check 2: the air filter (the #1 real culprit)

If the thermostat's fine, the filter is the next stop and, honestly, the cause most of the time. A clogged filter strangles airflow, the heat exchanger overheats, and a safety switch shuts the burners off to protect the unit — while the fan keeps running. Result: cold air.

Pull the filter. If you can't see light through it, replace it (they're about $15). Then give the furnace a few minutes to reset and try again. This is also why we nag everyone about maintenance: a quick [seasonal HVAC tune-up](/services/ac-repair) catches the filter, the flame sensor, and a dozen other small things before they become a cold night. ENERGY STAR has a simple [heating maintenance checklist](https://www.energystar.gov/products/heating_cooling) if you want to stay ahead of it.

## Check 3: power, pilot, and gas

Still cold? Run through these:

- **Power:** check the furnace switch (looks like a light switch nearby) and the breaker. Cycle it off, wait 30 seconds, on — *once*.
- **Gas:** make sure the gas valve to the furnace is open and other gas appliances work.
- **Pilot/igniter:** older units have a standing pilot light that can blow out; newer ones use an electronic igniter or hot-surface igniter that wears out. If the pilot won't stay lit or the igniter never glows, that's your problem.

A quick safety note that matters more than your comfort: if you ever **smell gas**, stop troubleshooting, leave the house, and call from outside. The U.S. Department of Energy has solid background on [how furnaces and heating systems work](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/furnaces-and-boilers) if you want to understand what you're looking at.

## When to call a technician

Call a pro when:

- It fires up, then shuts off and blows cold within a minute or two (likely a **flame sensor**, igniter, or limit-switch fault).
- You've replaced the filter and reset it, and it still won't heat.
- You see **soot, a yellow flame** (it should be crisp blue), or you smell anything off.
- It's dangerously cold and you've got kids, elderly family, or pets at home.
