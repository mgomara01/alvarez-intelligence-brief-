Your toilet has been hissing longer than most celebrity marriages, and you've started to take it personally. Good news: a toilet that **keeps running** is one of the cheapest, fastest fixes in the entire house — usually a $20 part and ten minutes — and you're probably not calling anyone tonight.

Let's open the tank (yes, the lid comes off, it's not load-bearing) and find the culprit.

## First, the good news: it's usually the flapper

Inside the tank, the **flapper** is the rubber seal at the bottom that lifts when you flush and drops to seal the tank afterward. Over time it warps, stiffens, or gets a film of mineral gunk — and then it stops sealing. Water keeps trickling into the bowl, the tank keeps refilling, and you get that endless hiss.

Quick test: drop a few drops of food coloring into the *tank* (not the bowl) and wait 10 minutes without flushing. If color shows up in the bowl, your flapper is leaking. Mystery solved.

## The three usual suspects

When a toilet keeps running, it's almost always one of these:

1. **A bad flapper** — worn, warped, or not seating flat. (Most common by a mile.)
2. **The chain** — too tight and it holds the flapper open; too loose and it tangles under the flapper.
3. **The fill valve or float set too high** — water rises above the overflow tube and drains continuously, so the valve never shuts off.

## The 10-minute flapper fix

1. Turn off the water at the shutoff valve behind the toilet (clockwise).
2. Flush to empty the tank.
3. Unhook the old flapper and chain, take it to the hardware store, and match it (or grab a universal one — about $5–$15).
4. Clip the new flapper on, hook up the chain with a *little* slack, turn the water back on, and test.

That's it. We do this so often that we tell people: before you Google "plumber," Google "where's the food coloring." Most running toilets never need us.

## If it's not the flapper: the fill valve and float

Still running? Look at the **water level**. It should sit about an inch below the top of the overflow tube. If water is spilling *into* the tube, the float is set too high — bend or clip it down (older ballcock) or twist the adjustment screw (modern float-cup valve). If the fill valve hisses constantly even with the level correct, the valve itself is worn; a replacement fill valve is about $15–$25 and swaps out in 15 minutes.

## When to actually call a plumber

Call a pro when:

- You've replaced the flapper **and** the fill valve and it *still* runs.
- There's water on the floor around the base (a failing wax ring or tank-to-bowl gasket).
- The tank or bowl is cracked.
- Multiple toilets do it at once — that can hint at a supply or pressure problem.
