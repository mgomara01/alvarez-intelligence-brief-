It's 96 degrees, the humidity could be classified as a liquid, and your AC is making all the right noises while delivering exactly none of the cold. In Florida, an **AC not cooling the house** isn't a minor inconvenience — it's a countdown. The good news: a few of the most common causes are things you can check yourself in five minutes before you ever call anyone.

Let's find out whether you've got a two-minute fix or a "yeah, that's ours" situation.

## First, the two-minute checks

Before anything dramatic:

- **Thermostat:** set to COOL, fan on AUTO (not ON — on ON, the fan blows room-temp air between cooling cycles and you swear the AC died).
- **Air filter:** pull it. If you can't see light through it, that's very likely your whole problem. A clogged filter chokes airflow, which leads straight to the next issue.
- **Breaker:** the outdoor condenser often has its own breaker. If it tripped, reset it once.

## The Florida classic: a frozen coil

Here's the one we get called about constantly. A dirty filter or low refrigerant restricts airflow, the evaporator coil drops below freezing, and ice forms — which insulates the coil so it cools even *less*. Now you've got an AC running full blast and blowing barely-cool air.

If you see **ice on the refrigerant lines or the indoor coil**, turn the system off at the thermostat and let it thaw completely (give it a few hours). Then swap the filter and try again. If it freezes up again, the cause is deeper — usually low refrigerant from a leak — and that needs our [AC repair & HVAC service](/services/ac-repair), because handling refrigerant requires EPA certification and the right tools.

## The other Florida classic: a clogged condensate drain

Your AC pulls a *staggering* amount of water out of Florida air, and that water drains away through a condensate line. When that line clogs with algae (and in this humidity, it will), the safety float switch shuts the system off — or worse, water backs up around your air handler. If your AC quit and there's water near the indoor unit, this is your prime suspect. A wet/dry vac on the drain line clears minor clogs; recurring ones mean it's time for a proper cleaning.

## Low refrigerant, dirty condenser, or a bad capacitor

If the basics check out and it's still not cooling, you're likely looking at one of these:

- **Low refrigerant** — almost always a leak, not "needs a top-up." It needs finding and fixing, not just refilling.
- **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — caked in Florida lawn debris, it can't dump heat. Gently rinse it (power off first).
- **Failing capacitor** — the outdoor fan or compressor won't start, often with a faint hum or click. Common, and a quick pro fix.

The U.S. Department of Energy has a solid primer on [how central air conditioners work](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/central-air-conditioning), and ENERGY STAR's [AC maintenance guidance](https://www.energystar.gov/products/heating_cooling) is worth a look if you want to keep yours from quitting in the first place.

Call a pro when the system freezes repeatedly, blows warm air, leaks refrigerant, makes grinding/buzzing noises, or just can't keep up after you've done the basics.
