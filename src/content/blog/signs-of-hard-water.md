You did the dishes, ran the dishwasher, and somehow the glasses came out looking *worse* — cloudy, spotted, like they'd been through a dust storm. Meanwhile your showerhead is wearing a crust you could chip off with a screwdriver, and your skin feels like parchment. Congratulations: you've been collecting the **signs of hard water in your house**, and in Tampa Bay, you've got plenty of company.

Here's how to confirm it, why Florida water is the way it is, and what actually fixes it.

## The 7 telltale signs

Run down this list. If three or more sound like your house, you've got hard water:

1. **White scale on faucets and showerheads** — that crusty buildup is mineral deposit (limescale).
2. **Spotty, cloudy dishes** — especially glassware straight from the dishwasher.
3. **Soap that won't lather** — body wash, shampoo, dish soap all fighting you.
4. **Dry, itchy skin and dull hair** — that "tight" feeling after every shower.
5. **Stiff, scratchy laundry** — and colors that fade faster.
6. **Lower water pressure over time** — scale narrowing the inside of pipes and fixtures.
7. **Water heaters and appliances dying early** — scale is brutal on anything that heats water.

That last one is the expensive one. (If your pressure is dropping for *other* reasons, our guide on a [plumber for low water pressure](/blog/plumber-for-low-water-pressure) covers those too.)

## Why Tampa Bay water is so hard

Hard water just means water carrying a lot of dissolved **calcium and magnesium**. Tampa Bay's supply draws heavily from Florida groundwater that's been sitting in limestone aquifers — and limestone is basically calcium. The water picks up those minerals on the way to your tap. It's perfectly safe to drink; both the [USGS hard-water explainer](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/hardness-water) and the [EPA's drinking-water standards](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/drinking-water-regulations-and-contaminants) confirm the minerals aren't a health concern. They're just relentless on your plumbing.

## What hard water is quietly costing you

Hard water doesn't break things overnight — it taxes them. Scale builds up inside your water heater so it burns more energy and wears out years early. It clogs aerators and showerheads. It makes you use more soap, more shampoo, more detergent. None of it is dramatic on its own, but it adds up to real money and constant low-grade annoyance.
