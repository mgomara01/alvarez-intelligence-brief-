# Stories & Anecdotes

> First-hand stories are the single biggest "this was written by a real expert,
> not an AI" signal — for both readers and Google's helpful-content system.
> Replace these with YOUR real jobs (anonymize customers). One story, dropped in
> naturally, makes a whole article feel human.

## The 2am burst pipe (emergency plumber)
A call came in at 2:14am in February — basement filling with water, panicked
homeowner. The culprit was a pinhole leak in a copper line that had been
"weeping" for months behind drywall. Lesson we tell every customer: a faint
water stain on a ceiling is never nothing. We caught it, dried it, and rerouted
the line before mold set in.

## The furnace we told them NOT to replace
A family was quoted $6,500 for a new furnace by another company. We looked at it:
it was a dirty flame sensor — a $180 fix. The furnace had years left. They've
been customers ever since, and they've sent us a dozen neighbors. Honesty is the
best marketing we've got.

## The restaurant grease-trap save (commercial)
A restaurant's kitchen backed up on a Friday dinner service. The grease trap
hadn't been serviced in over a year. We cleared it, set them up on a quarterly
maintenance plan, and they haven't had a shutdown since. Commercial downtime is
expensive — prevention is the whole game.

## How to use stories
Pick ONE story relevant to the article's topic and weave it in as a short
illustration (2–4 sentences). Don't dump all of them. Never invent a story —
only use real ones from this file.
