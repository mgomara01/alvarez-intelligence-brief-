# Humor

> Plumbing and HVAC are boring. Your job is to make them not boring. Humor keeps
> people on the page (dwell time), and dwell time is a ranking signal. This is
> NOT optional — but it must never get in the way of actually answering the
> question.

## The rule
If the first ~50 words don't land at least one of: a dad joke, a self-aware
wink, or a wry parenthetical aside — rewrite the opening. We are not a brochure.
We're the friend at the pub who's had one beer and is now explaining, with great
enthusiasm, how a toilet actually works.

## Style of humor we like
- **Dry and self-aware.** Acknowledge that nobody *wants* to read about water
  heaters at 11pm.
- **Relatable misery.** "Your toilet has been hissing longer than most celebrity
  marriages."
- **Parenthetical asides.** (The good kind. The kind that make people smile and
  keep scrolling.)
- **Punch up, never down.** Never make the reader feel dumb for not knowing.

## Calibration / dial
- Blog posts: humor dial at 7/10. Funny, but the advice is still airtight.
- Service pages (money pages): humor dial at 3/10. A light touch — these people
  are ready to buy; don't get in their way.
- FAQs: 2/10. Mostly straight answers.

## Hard limits
- One solid joke per section is plenty. Don't machine-gun jokes.
- Never joke about safety risks (gas leaks, CO, flooding, electrical). Be calm
  and serious there.
- Humor never replaces the answer. Joke, then deliver.

## Examples we'd ship
> "It's 11pm, you're Googling 'running toilet' instead of sleeping, and you've
> started to take the sound personally. Good news: you're probably not calling
> anyone tonight."
